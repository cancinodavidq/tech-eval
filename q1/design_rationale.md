# Design Rationale: Vanilla RAG as the Starting Point

This design intentionally starts with a vanilla RAG architecture rather than a feature-rich system. The reasoning is straightforward: before adding complexity, you need a working baseline you can measure against.

## On Business Assumptions

The requirements as stated — 500 concurrent users, p95 latency under 3 seconds, 200,000 documents updated weekly — were reviewed against typical usage patterns for internal legal and compliance tools in enterprise environments. These figures may actually overestimate real-world demand. Internal legal teams rarely generate uniform, sustained concurrency; usage tends to spike around regulatory cycles and remains low otherwise. A system sized and engineered for peak theoretical load from day one carries unnecessary cost and operational overhead before the product has proven its value or its actual usage shape is understood.

## What the Design Covers

The vanilla architecture addresses every stated functional requirement:

- **Hybrid retrieval** — semantic search (embeddings, weight 0.7) combined with keyword search (BM25, weight 0.3)
- **Metadata filtering** — top-k chunk selection after retrieval merge
- **Page-level citation** — inline citations generated by the LLM, evaluated via ALCE
- **Source grounding** — answers tied directly to retrieved documents; unanswerable queries rejected rather than hallucinated
- **Online evaluation loop** — RAGAS (precision, recall, MAP) and ALCE (citation precision and recall) running over real traffic
- **Azure-native deployment** — clean integration with existing SSO (Azure AD) and audit logging for compliance

What the design does not include: a reranker, a query router, multi-hop reasoning, or other retrieval enhancements. These are not excluded because they are wrong. They are excluded because they should be introduced in response to measured gaps, not in anticipation of them.

## The Role of the Evaluation Layer

The evaluation layer — RAGAS and ALCE — is not optional decoration. It is the mechanism that makes iteration possible. Without it, any subsequent change (adding a reranker, adjusting chunking strategy, tuning the hybrid weight) is engineering by intuition. With it, every change has a before/after comparison grounded in real traffic and real queries.

## Design Philosophy

Build the simplest system that meets the stated requirements. Instrument it properly. Let observed performance drive what gets added next.

This is not a constraint on ambition. It is how you avoid building a system that is expensive to maintain, difficult to diagnose, and poorly matched to what users actually need — before you have the data to know what they actually need.
